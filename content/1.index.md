I'm the Lead Engineer at [Happy Dog](https://happydog.digital), where I set technical direction across more than forty production applications — web platforms spanning PHP, Go, and JavaScript services behind containerized infrastructure, with mobile apps fronting some of them. My heaviest day-to-day is backend PHP: Symfony, with a particular focus on Sylius e-commerce, and Laravel. But the work routinely reaches into other languages, into the pipelines and infrastructure that hold everything together, and into the conversations that turn client problems into shipped software.

My career started in the U.S. Army in 2005, where I spent nine years as an Information Technology Specialist. As a Data Support Team Chief I led the team responsible for the deployable network infrastructure a unit takes into the field, and I finished as a Senior Information Systems Support Specialist with information assurance security duties on top of that. As a Sergeant, I was directly responsible for the morale, welfare, and professional development of a team of junior Soldiers — a part of the job I still draw on when leading engineering work today. Twenty-one years on, the infrastructure has moved from satellite terminals in a field to Kubernetes clusters in Google Cloud, and the mix of operations, security, and people work is the through-line.

Open source is where I cut my teeth as a developer, and it's still where most of my public work lives. I maintain Pagerfanta, a PHP pagination library that 289 other packages depend on. I spent about a decade in the Joomla project — coordinating four feature releases, building its framework, serving on and later leading its security team, and sitting on the Board of Directors of Open Source Matters, Inc., the not-for-profit behind it. I was also one of the lead developers on Mautic through its 1.0 release. There's a fuller account on [the open source page](/open-source), and the packages themselves are documented at [babdev.com](https://www.babdev.com).
